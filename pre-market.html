C. Macro News (Detailed)

1. The Fed held — and the market repriced toward hikes, not cuts

The FOMC held the target range at 3.75–4.00% on October 28 in a 10–2 vote, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissenting in favour of an immediate +25bp hike. The statement called activity "expanding at a strong pace" and inflation "elevated". Chair Warsh declined to commit to a December move, and said that from 2027 one meeting per year will carry no rate vote or statement. December hike odds are now priced at ~83–85%, and some banks now price two further hikes in the first half of 2027. Do not frame this as rate-cut expectations: the 24-hour story is hike risk, not cuts. Source: Admiral Markets · Source: CaixaBank Research

2. Alphabet failed the market's new test; Microsoft passed it

Alphabet Q3: revenue $127.6 billion (+25%), just below the ~$128 billion consensus; GAAP EPS $2.12 versus ~$3.02 expected — including an ~$8.2–8.7 billion mark-to-market loss on equity investments, attributed to SpaceX — with net income down ~25% to ~$26.2 billion; adjusted EPS $2.67 also missed. Google Cloud revenue more than doubled (+103%) to $30.7 billion with operating profit $11.1 billion, but search and other advertising grew only 12% (down from 17%), YouTube ads $11.2 billion missed ~$11.4 billion, and advertising revenue ~$82 billion missed ~$83.4 billion. Capital spending hit $51.4 billion in the quarter, free cash flow was negative $9.07 billion — a second straight negative quarter — and management kept 2026 capex at $195–205 billion while saying 2027 spending will "increase significantly". Shares fell ~9% after hours and ~10–10.5% pre-market, wiping roughly $400 billion of market value by one Reuters-based account. Source: OQLIV · Source: InvestingLive

Microsoft fiscal Q1 2027: revenue $92.3 billion (+19%), adjusted EPS $4.89 versus ~$4.73 expected, Azure +48% to $33.2 billion, Microsoft Cloud $63.9 billion (+30%), commercial remaining performance obligations $677 billion (+73%). Capital spending including finance leases was $45.2 billion — below the ~$50 billion projection because third-party capacity deliveries slipped, not because spending was cut — and free cash flow was $18.6 billion, roughly $10 billion above expectations. Guidance: Q2 revenue $96–97.1 billion, next-quarter Azure growth 49–50%, capex above $50 billion next quarter, and calendar-2026 capex ~$170 billion. Shares rose ~3–4% after hours and +2.6–3.7% pre-market. The market's test is now explicit: spending is not disqualifying, but the cash has to keep flowing while it happens. Source: TechFyle · Source: Reuters

3. Samsung's record quarter disappointed — and the KOSPI round trip is the warning

Samsung reported Q3 revenue of 195.25 trillion won (+126.9%) and operating profit of 107.44 trillion won (+783.1%) — a fourth straight record quarter and the first tech company to pass 100 trillion won in quarterly operating profit — with the chip division contributing 106.5 trillion won and memory revenue up 427% on HBM4 sales more than tripling quarter over quarter. But net profit of 75.14 trillion won missed expectations (~89 trillion per one account), and the company deferred details of the remaining 55–75 trillion won of its 90–110 trillion won 2026 shareholder-return plan to a late-January 2027 board meeting (35 trillion won already planned or done, including a 30 trillion won Q3 dividend of 4,601 won per share). Shares fell more than 5% intraday (intraday low −6.2%) and closed down ~4–5%; the KOSPI touched 6,100.11 intraday, then fell to 5,817.92 before closing at 5,937.66 (−1.24%). SK Hynix, by contrast, rose ~2.6% in Seoul to 1,526,000 won. The lesson for leveraged Korea exposure: a record print is not a floor when the return plan is the trade. Source: Morningstar / Dow Jones · Source: KimKJ market briefing
