B. 30-Second Conclusion

Futures are modestly higher into the biggest week of the quarter, but this is a relief bounce inside an unresolved repricing — not a clean all-clear. Friday's selloff broadened beyond tech (defensives fell too), oil spiked to $116.72 Brent on Middle East headlines and is only partly retracing, and the market's real test starts Wednesday: the FOMC decision plus Microsoft and Alphabet earnings after the close, with SK Hynix earnings the same day. The AI-spending question has flipped from "how fast can they build?" to "who pays, with what cash flow, and at what borrowing cost?" — and this week's hyperscaler prints are where that question gets answered. Until then, the tradeable regime is: long-volatility respect (VIX ~19.5), tight spreads, no heroics into Wednesday's close.

F. Short-Option Strategy — Read-Only Analysis (Not a Trade Order)

Read-only, no trading action taken. This chapter is strategy analysis only, following the standing playbook (spreads only, never naked short puts/calls; single-trade max loss 1–2% of account; DTE 14–30 days; exit at 50–70% of credit). Direction follows market state, not a fixed annual bias. For anything only the broker app can confirm — positions, expiry, assignment, margin — check the IBKR app; nothing here asserts it.

Regime read: elevated vol + event wall = wider wings, farther strikes, smaller size

VIX 19.45 with realized vol only ~10–12% means the market is paying for event risk (FOMC + MSFT/GOOG Wednesday, GDP/PCE Thursday, SNDK Thursday) that has not yet arrived. That is a seller-friendly vol backdrop in principle — but a dangerous one to lean into ahead of binary prints. The playbook discipline for this week: if you sell premium at all, sell it after the event wall where possible, or size it so a Wednesday gap does not matter. Selling the rip into Wednesday's close, rather than selling strength on Monday morning, is the cleaner expression of the same view.

KORU — default remains bear call spread (playbook)

KOSPI −1.46% Monday, foreign selling, and Samsung/SK Hynix earnings plus the Fed on Wednesday = textbook overnight-gap risk (playbook: 30-day vol 134.2%, annualized 192.7%, 63% of days move >±5%, historic worst day −41.9%, and an Oct. 15-style overnight gap is a lived risk). Default: bear call spread, 10–15 delta far out-of-the-money, $3–5 wing width, 14–30 DTE, sold on a rip — not chased after a gap down. Do not run cash-secured puts / wheel on a decaying 3x (assignment = catching a falling leveraged product). Before any first trade in a new expiry, confirm the listed months and strike spacing in the IBKR app; do not assume weeklies exist. Reduce or tighten ahead of known Korea events (Samsung/SK Hynix prints, peninsula headlines).
