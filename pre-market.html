B. 30-Second Conclusion

Wednesday is the hinge day of the week, and the tape knows it: futures are mixed, memory is on fire, and everything else is waiting. SK Hynix's record quarter — revenue over 100 trillion won for the first time, operating profit up more than 7× — plus a ~100 trillion won shareholder-return plan sent the KOSPI through 6,000 and lifted KORU ~12% and the whole U.S. memory complex pre-market. But the operating beat comes with a net-profit miss, and the real binary events are still ahead: the Fed decision at 14:00 ET, then Microsoft and Alphabet after the close. Today's move is positioning into those prints, not resolution of them. Keep spreads wide, strikes far, and size for a post-Fed, post-earnings gap — not for this morning's memory bounce.

C. Macro News (Detailed)

1. Fed day: a hold is priced; December and Warsh's tone are the trade

The FOMC decision lands today at 14:00 ET, with Chair Warsh's press conference at 14:30 ET. The current target range is 3.75–4.00% after September's hike. A hold is overwhelmingly expected — ~90–94% priced, and a Reuters survey found 94 of 100 economists expecting no change, with all 100 forecasting another hike in December. Futures price roughly 80–83% for a December +25bp move. This is a non-SEP meeting (no dot plot), so Warsh's words and any dissents carry the signal. September data frame the dilemma: CPI accelerated to 3.7% year over year while payrolls rose just 29,000. Source: EBC · Source: PipTheory

2. SK Hynix rewrites the memory story — operating beat, net miss, huge returns

SK Hynix reported Q3 revenue of 104.6155 trillion won (+327.9% year over year, its first quarter above 100 trillion) and operating profit of 83.3334 trillion won (up ~631%, ~80% margin), beating the LSEG SmartEstimate of ~76.7 trillion. It also announced ~100 trillion won (~$75 billion) of 2026 shareholder returns — more than 30 trillion in dividends plus more than 60 trillion in buybacks/cancellations, including the 40 trillion buyback announced in August; newly approved items include a 14 trillion dividend (20,000 won/share, half of it a special dividend from Kioxia gains) and a 20 trillion buyback. It said HBM4 shipments and revenue will surpass HBM3E in Q4 and that it expects demand to exceed memory supply through 2030, with long-term agreements covering more than half of 2027–28 capacity. Do not call it a clean beat: net profit of 39.7756 trillion won missed a FactSet consensus of ~61.4 trillion. Operating-line headlines say "beat"; net-line headlines say "miss" — both are true on different lines. Source: Korea JoongAng Daily · Source: The Northern Current

3. Rates, dollar, gold — 10Y testing 5%, dollar at a 2-week high

The 10Y sits just at/above 5.00% Wednesday morning (Tuesday close 4.99%); the 2Y is ~4.70–4.74%. DXY is ~102.4, a two-week high. Gold slipped through Wednesday morning to ~$4,009–4,051 after closing Tuesday at ~$4,073–4,075 (+0.9–1.1%). Tuesday's $70 billion 5-year auction priced at 4.845% with bid-to-cover 2.33 and indirect bidders at 60% — firmer than Monday's soft $69 billion 2-year sale. The rate story into the Fed is therefore "high and sticky", not "falling into the decision". Source: Market Briefer

4. Oil: a claimed refinery strike re-inflates the risk premium

WTI fell ~1.8% Tuesday to $99.90 on Tehran-diplomacy hopes, then rose back above $101–101.90 Wednesday morning (Brent ~$115.60–115.90). The trigger was a claim by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis that they hit Saudi Arabia's largest refinery (reported as Ras Tanura, ~550,000 bpd) with a missile on Tuesday. Unconfirmed: Saudi authorities had not confirmed the strike at the time of writing — the oil spike is on the claim plus the risk premium, not on a confirmed outage. Source: Duke FM / Reuters wire

5. The AI financing debate sharpens: vendors funding their customer

Nvidia will invest $3 billion and AMD $1 billion in Meta's equity offering (Meta raising up to $97.5 billion for AI infrastructure; aggregate cornerstone interest up to $5 billion in the common portion per filings), while Meta deploys Nvidia Vera Rubin systems, Vera CPUs and AMD Helios systems for Muse models. Critics call it circular — vendors funding their largest customer; bulls call it demand insurance. Separately, The Information reported OpenAI plans to buy Google TPUs. Unconfirmed: no terms, and neither company confirmed it. Broadcom's $63 billion Anthropic financing package (announced October 26) remains the other leg of the same debate. Source: The Jo AI · Source: BusinessWorld / The Information

F. Short-Option Strategy — Read-Only Analysis (Not a Trade Order)

Read-only, no trading action taken. This chapter is strategy analysis only, following the standing playbook (spreads only, never naked short puts/calls; single-trade max loss 1–2% of account; DTE 14–30 days; exit at 50–70% of credit). Direction follows market state, not a fixed annual bias. For anything only the broker app can confirm — positions, expiry, assignment, margin — check the IBKR app; nothing here asserts it.

Regime read: the Fed and two megacap prints land inside one session — sell premium for today, not for this morning

VIX 17.52 after a −4.7% day looks calm, but the options market disagrees with any "calm" label: MSFT ±5.09%, GOOG ±5.94–5.99%, LABU ±~10% and UVXY ±7.24% into Friday's expiry are all large priced moves. Selling premium this morning means collecting Tuesday's vol for Wednesday's three binary events (Fed 14:00 ET, MSFT/GOOG after the close). The playbook discipline for today, unchanged from the week: if you sell premium at all, size it so a post-Fed, post-earnings gap does not matter — or wait for the post-print vol crush, which is the cleaner premium to harvest. Selling the rip after the prints, rather than chasing this morning's memory-led bounce, is the cleaner expression of the same view.

KORU — default remains bear call spread (playbook), and today proves why in both directions

KORU closed Tuesday +8.91% to $11.86 and gapped to $13.30 (+12.43%) pre-market on the KOSPI surge — after falling ~23% over the prior 7 days. That is a two-way gap regime, not a trend: annualised vol 192.7%, 30-day vol 134.2%, 63% of days move >±5%, historic worst day −41.9%. A +12% pre-market print is a rip to sell into, not strength to chase with a put spread: default remains bear call spread, 10–15 delta far out-of-the-money, $3–5 wing width, 14–30 DTE. Do not run cash-secured puts / wheel on a decaying 3x. Before any first trade in a new expiry, confirm the listed months and strike spacing in the IBKR app; do not assume weeklies exist. Note the SK Hynix event is now behind us — the next Korea overnight risk is Samsung's Q3 print Thursday (Seoul) and any peninsula headlines.

LABU — the calmer 3x; put-spread side is the trend side, with no new signal today

LABU's vol regime (annualised 89.5%, worst day −12.9%, only 7.6% of days beyond ±10%) is roughly half KORU's, and all components trade in U.S. hours — no overnight gap. Tuesday's +8.39% to $242.25 extends the biotech bounce, but the October 30 options market still prices ±~10% in two days, so "calmer" does not mean quiet. Framework unchanged: 15–20 delta strikes, slightly narrower wings than KORU, 14–30 DTE, exit at 50–70% of credit. With no fresh LABU catalyst today and the Fed wall at 14:00 ET, there is no new reason to add size before the prints.

SOXL / TECL / TQQQ — the memory bounce is the event, not the entry

Apply LABU-level parameters (wings slightly narrower than KORU), direction by each underlying's trend and vol state. SOXL +3.02% pre-market stacks on Tuesday's +11.69%; TQQQ +1.37% tracks Nasdaq-futures strength. Both share tonight's clock (MSFT/GOOG), and SOXL adds Thursday's SNDK print. A 3x fund gapping on a single earnings report is not a floor. If selling premium into that wall, keep strikes far, wings wide enough to survive a ±1-day gap, and size small; otherwise wait for the post-print crush.

UVXY — only short-DTE bear call spreads, or avoid

Volatility products decay differently from equity 3x ETFs: contango is the headwind, and a VIX fade post-Fed is the friend. Playbook: only short-DTE bear call spreads, or avoid entirely. A long-volatility hedge via UVXY is a different trade from selling UVXY premium — do not confuse the two. With VIX at 17.52 into a Fed day, the fade case exists only if Warsh sounds patient; a hawkish surprise inverts it fast.
