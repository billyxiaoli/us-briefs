B. 30-Second Conclusion

Wednesday was a sorting day, not a rally day. Memory stocks did the heavy lifting — SK Hynix up 13% in the U.S., KORU up 16%, Micron up 6%, SanDisk up 8% — the Fed did exactly what was priced (hold at 3.75–4.00%, two dissents wanting a hike), and then the after-hours tape drew the line the market had been waiting for: Microsoft beat on Azure and jumped about 5%, Alphabet missed on earnings and fell about 7–8%. The AI trade is no longer being bought as a basket; it is being graded name by name. Thursday brings the other half of the exam — Q3 GDP and September PCE at 08:30 ET, then Amazon, Intel and SanDisk after the close. Keep spreads wide and strikes far into that tape; a single-cloud-print gap is now a demonstrated, not theoretical, risk.

C. Macro Day Review (Detailed)

1. Fed holds at 3.75–4.00%, 10–2, with two dissents for a hike

The FOMC left the federal funds target range unchanged at 3.75–4.00% at 14:00 ET, one month after September's quarter-point hike — the first hike in three years. The vote was 10–2: Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented, preferring another quarter-point increase. The statement called activity "expanding at a strong pace", said inflation "remains elevated", and repeated that accumulating more information will help determine the stance "appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee's 2 percent goal". Chair Kevin Warsh said politics played no role and that the coming period is "information-rich"; traders raised December hike odds to roughly 80% (CME FedWatch). Source: USA Today · Source: Reuters · Source: Launchpad Digital

2. Warsh restructures the calendar: 8 meetings, only 6 rate decisions from 2027

Warsh announced that from 2027 one of the FOMC's eight annual meetings will be a strategy-only session with no rate vote, and a second strategy-only meeting follows in 2028 — cutting rate-setting meetings from eight to six per year. The move, framed as a historic overhaul, landed on the same day inflation printed 3.7% year over year for September (core 2.6%, PPI above 6%). Treasury Secretary Bessent said another hike "would not necessarily be a mistake" and that bond markets have already delivered substantial tightening. Source: Barchart · Source: Zacks · Source: DayTradingPrep

3. Rates and the dollar: 10Y parked above 5%, dollar eased

The 10Y yield spent the day around 4.99–5.03%, crossing 5% after the decision, with the 2Y near 4.70–4.74%. DXY eased 0.18% to 102.14 after touching a two-week high near 102.4 in the morning. The read: the bond market, not the Fed statement, is doing the tightening — which is exactly why a December hike is priced near 80% even after a hold. Gold held around $4,070 and gold miners (GDX) rose 0.91%.

4. Oil: EIA draw plus an unconfirmed refinery claim kept crude near $103

WTI rose about 2.9% intraday to near $103 after the EIA reported a 1.9-million-barrel drop in commercial crude inventories (gasoline inventories rose 3.9 million barrels); Brent traded near $115. The overhang remains the Houthi claim of a missile hit on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery (~550,000 bpd). Unconfirmed: Saudi authorities had not confirmed the strike — the premium is on the claim, not a confirmed outage. September's goods deficit printed $134.9 billion; wholesale inventories rose 0.6%, retail inventories 0.3%. Source: CAMS

5. The AI financing debate sharpened into the prints

Into the close, the tape kept repricing the circularity question: Nvidia ($3B) and AMD ($1B) investing in Meta's equity raise while Meta buys their systems; Broadcom's $63B Anthropic financing package; and a report that OpenAI plans to buy Google TPUs. Unconfirmed: the OpenAI-TPU report is unconfirmed by either company. Wednesday's after-hours verdict — Microsoft rewarded, Alphabet punished — is the market's first real answer to that debate: spending is fine when it converts to profit, and punished when it does not. Source: Zacks · Source: TheStreet Pro

6. Geopolitics and the calendar overhang

Reports that Washington asked Gulf states to prepare for a possible new campaign against Iran after the November 3 midterms kept energy and defence risk premia elevated, and the Fed explicitly cited uncertainty "owing, in part, to geopolitical developments". The midterms themselves (Nov. 3) are now the next political binary; Warsh went out of his way to say the decision had "nothing to do" with politics. Global bond issuance hit a record $6.61 trillion for the year.

F. After-Hours News

Microsoft beats — Azure +48%, stock +4–5%

Microsoft reported fiscal Q1 2027 revenue of $92.28 billion (consensus ~$90.6–90.7B) and adjusted EPS of $4.89 (consensus ~$4.72–4.73), with Azure revenue up 48% (estimate 44.8%) and total Microsoft Cloud revenue $63.9 billion (+30%). Operating income was $45.9 billion, net income $36.6 billion, and the OpenAI investment contributed a $207 million net gain; $10.2 billion was returned via dividends and buybacks. Capex of $39.4 billion came in below a ~$45.9 billion consensus in one report — though another report puts capex-plus-finance-leases at $45.2 billion (+30%), so treat the capex comparison as vendor-dependent (see §I). Shares traded to $556.66 (+4.83%) in extended trading. Zacks also notes ~60,000 Microsoft employees are eligible for severance packages, suggesting a new round of job cuts. Source: MarketBeat · Source: Market Flux · Source: Zacks

Alphabet misses — EPS $2.12, stock −7 to −8%

Alphabet reported Q3 revenue of $127.6 billion (estimate ~$128.2B) and EPS of $2.12, far below a ~$3.01–3.03 consensus and $2.87 a year ago, hurt by an $8.7 billion loss on equity securities that swung other income to −$8.2 billion from +$12.8 billion a year earlier. Search revenue ($63.4B) and YouTube ads ($11.2B) both missed, and free cash flow was −$9.1 billion after roughly $51.4 billion of quarterly capex. The offsets were real: Cloud revenue more than doubled (+103%) to $30.7 billion, Search query growth was the fastest in five years, and the Gemini app reached a billion users. Alphabet issued $28.7 billion in senior notes, sold $12.4 billion of stock through its at-the-market program, and suspended buybacks for the quarter. Shares fell about 7% immediately after the print (about −8.1% on one after-hours board). This is only Alphabet's second earnings miss in five years. Source: Market Flux · Source: TheStreet Pro · Source: CTT

G. Short-Option Strategy Review (analysis only — read-only, no trading action taken)

Regime read: Vol compressed into a resolved event (VIX 16.91, −3.48%) while realised vol exploded in one pocket (memory/Korea) and earnings vol repriced name-by-name after the bell. That combination — calm index vol, violent single-name gaps — is exactly the regime where short spreads earn their keep on the index side and get hurt on the single-name side. The playbook's iron rules still bind: spreads only, never naked; max loss 1–2% per trade; 14–30 DTE; take 50–70% of credit and leave.

KORU — the day's warning and opportunity in one print

KORU +16.19% to $13.78 in a single session is a textbook 3x gap: the KOSPI move plus leverage, with the playbook's overnight-gap risk now visible in the other direction (a +16% day can reverse just as fast on a Seoul reversal or a Samsung headline). Default stance stays bear call spread per the playbook — sell the rip, not the crash — and a day like today is the rip to sell into, with wide $3–5 wings and far out-of-the-money strikes, rather than chasing the move. Known overnight event risk is live: Seoul opens hours after this brief, and Thursday's U.S. data can gap KORU again at the U.S. open. Size for a ±10% day, because 35% of KORU's trading days historically exceed ±10%.

LABU / biotech — trend intact, spreads can stay two-sided but respect the tape

LABU +4.91% extends a strong year (the playbook notes YTD strength and a +60.8% month in June as the cautionary tale for call spreads). In a trending tape, put spreads keep the wind at their back; call spreads remain the side that gets run over. 15–20 delta strikes, slightly narrower wings than KORU, remain the framework — but Thursday's GDP/PCE is a sector-agnostic vol event, so new put spreads opened into it should be smaller than usual or deferred until after 08:30 ET.

SOXL / TECL / TQQQ — chip vol is the trade; index vol is the calm

SOXL +6.20% and TECL +2.28% show chip vol expanding while TQQQ (+0.07%) shows Nasdaq vol flat — the spread between single-theme vol and index vol widened. For SOXL, the SNDK print Thursday after the close is a known vol event inside the ETF: short call spreads into it face gap risk from a stock that just rose 7.66% into its own earnings. For TQQQ, the flat day plus falling VIX argues for patience: put spreads need a pullback to find edge, and chasing a flat tape into GDP/PCE/Amazon is paying vol for someone else's event.

UVXY — do not fight the compression, do not chase it either

UVXY −2.97% on VIX −3.48% is contango plus vol crush working as designed. The playbook allows only short-DTE bear call spreads or avoidance here; a post-Fed vol crush is the worst time to initiate fresh short vol — the easy compression is already banked by the market, and Thursday's data can re-inflate vol faster than contango decays it.

Single names with live event risk

MSFT/GOOG: the after-hours gaps (+4.8% / −7 to −8%) reprice both names' vol surfaces overnight; spreads struck off Wednesday's closes are stale by Thursday's open — any existing short spread in either name must be re-marked before assuming its risk, and new spreads should wait for the Thursday regular session to set a range. SNDK: +7.66% into Thursday's print with ~±8.3% implied — a short spread here is an earnings bet, not a rent collection; wings must be wide enough to survive a two-sided gap. MU/NVDA/AMD: memory momentum argues against short calls into strength; the playbook's CXMT-supply-chain watch item remains live background risk for the storage complex.

Outlook — Thursday and next week

Thursday 08:30 ET (GDP advance, September PCE, jobless claims) is the next vol event, then Amazon/Intel/SanDisk after the close; Friday brings the Bank of Japan decision, ExxonMobil/Chevron before the open, the Employment Cost Index and Chicago PMI.
