B. 30-Second Conclusion

Thursday completed the exam Wednesday started — and the AI trade failed the second half. Alphabet fell 12.29% in the regular session on a revenue miss and negative $9.1 billion free cash flow, then Amazon missed on both lines and guided soft, Intel guided far below consensus, and SanDisk beat but faded after hours. C. Macro Day Review (Detailed)

1. Growth beat, inflation in line — and the Fed story did not soften

At 08:30 ET, Q3 GDP (advance) printed +3.4% annualized, above the 3.2% consensus and up from Q2's 2.2%, with consumer spending +3.8% and equipment investment +20.9%. September PCE printed headline +0.5% month over month / 3.6% year over year, core +0.3% / 3.1% — in line with Reuters-polled expectations. Jobless claims for the week ended Oct. 24 were 194,000 vs 199,000 expected. Futures pared losses after the data, but the combination — strong growth plus 3.1% core PCE — kept the December-hike narrative alive rather than easing it; December hike odds remained priced near 80–85% (CME FedWatch / LSEG). One characterisation conflict: Investopedia described PCE as "slightly cooler than expected", against Reuters' "in line" — the in-line read is used here. Source: Reuters via 98.5 The River · Source: Investopedia

2. ECB holds unanimously; Lagarde to step down in February 2027

The European Central Bank held its deposit rate at 2.50% (main refinancing 2.65%, marginal lending 2.90%) in a unanimous decision; the council debated a hike but chose to wait, with September eurozone inflation at 3.8% and energy inflation at 18.9%. President Christine Lagarde announced she will step down on February 7, 2027, about nine months early, and will still lead the December and February meetings. The euro fell to about $1.113, its lowest since May. Source: FXStreet · Source: Reuters

3. Rates fell, the dollar eased, gold slipped — no haven bid

The 10Y yield fell about 5.6 basis points to roughly 4.99% (CAMS reports 4.94% — the range 4.94–4.99% is used here, direction down confirmed). DXY eased 0.24% on the day. Gold settled near $4,031–4,039 (−0.31%), and gold miners fell much harder than gold itself (GDX −3.12%) — the day's risk-off money went to bonds and defensive equities, not to gold. WTI crude was essentially flat at $102.82 (+0.04%) despite the week's energy headlines.

4. Strait of Hormuz headlines: claim without confirmation

CAMS notes Iran's Revolutionary Guards said three tankers hit mines south of the Strait of Hormuz, with UK maritime officials separately reporting one vessel on fire without confirming cause. CAMS explicitly states sources did not establish a full Strait closure or a sustained oil-flow interruption. Unconfirmed: any closure claim — the oil market's flat close ($102.82) is consistent with a headline, not a confirmed supply shock.

5. The AI financing debate got its verdict — three times

Wednesday's question — does AI spending convert to profit? — was answered three more times on Thursday, all negatively: Alphabet (negative free cash flow on massive capex), Amazon (trailing free cash flow −$42.4 billion with a $220 billion 2026 capex plan reaffirmed), and Intel (weak guide plus a factory explosion). Microsoft remains the exception that proves the rule. The market is no longer debating AI spending in the abstract; it is repricing each spender's cash conversion, name by name.

G. Short-Option Strategy Review (analysis only — read-only, no trading action taken)

Regime read: Contained index vol (VIX ~17) with violent single-name gaps — GOOG −12.29% in one session, Amazon and Intel double-digit after-hours moves, SOXL −13% after hours. That combination is exactly the regime where short spreads earn their keep on the index side and get hurt on the single-name side. The playbook's iron rules still bind: spreads only, never naked; max loss 1–2% per trade; 14–30 DTE; take 50–70% of credit and leave.

KORU — a two-day round-trip is the warning

KORU +16.19% Wednesday, −7.62% Thursday to $12.73. That whipsaw is the playbook's overnight-gap risk made visible in both directions: a +16% day can reverse just as fast on a Seoul reversal or a Samsung headline. Default stance stays bear call spread — sell the rip, not the crash — with wide $3–5 wings and far out-of-the-money strikes, sized for a ±10% day (35% of KORU's trading days historically exceed ±10%). Note the KORU/KOSPI gap: −7.62% vs 3× the KOSPI day (−3.7% implied) — the MSCI Korea 25/50 USD index and currency sit between the two, so do not size KORU spreads off the KOSPI print alone. Known overnight event risk is live again: Seoul opens hours after this brief, and Friday's U.S. data can gap KORU again at the U.S. open.

LABU / biotech — the exception, but expiry vol is extreme

LABU +1.11% was the only 3x leveraged ETF green on the day, on the health-care rotation — and it still faded to $254.00 (−1.15%) after hours. In a trending tape, put spreads keep the wind at their back; but LABU options into the Oct. 30 expiry carried ~233.5% at-the-money implied vol with an expected move of ±$26.84 (±10.56%) per TipRanks. That is expiry-event vol, not rent: short spreads opened into Friday's expiry should be smaller than usual or deferred until after the expiry passes.

SOXL / TECL / TQQQ — chip vol is the trade; the gap is already in motion

SOXL −4.62% in the regular session plus ~−9% to −13% after hours on Intel contagion means Friday's chip open starts with a gap already priced in after-hours quotes — short call spreads struck off Thursday's closes are stale by Friday's open, and any existing short spread in SOXL/TECL/TQQQ must be re-marked before assuming its risk. For TQQQ, QQQ closed below its 10-day (720.6), 21-day (725.5) and 50-day (724.7) averages per Confluence's note — the Nasdaq trend leg is deteriorating even while the VIX stays calm. New put spreads need a pullback to find edge; chasing a falling tape into the BoJ decision and the Employment Cost Index is paying vol for someone else's event.

UVXY — do not fight the compression, do not chase it either

UVXY −3.35% on a day the Nasdaq fell 1.42% is the proof that this is not a vol event: the easy compression is already banked, and Friday's data (BoJ, ECI, Chicago PMI) can re-inflate vol faster than contango decays it. The playbook allows only short-DTE bear call spreads or avoidance here; a post-earnings vol crush is the worst time to initiate fresh short vol.

Single names with live event risk

GOOG: a −12.29% session reprices its vol surface overnight; spreads struck off Wednesday's closes are stale. MSFT/META: MSFT faded its beat, META rebounded — both names' vol now trades off Thursday's closes, not Wednesday's earnings gaps. SNDK: the beat-and-fade after hours (popped +3%, faded to −4.31%) is a two-sided gap in one evening — a short spread here was an earnings bet, not rent collection, and Friday's session must set a range before new exposure. MU/NVDA/AMD: memory momentum broke Thursday; the playbook's CXMT-supply-chain watch item remains live background risk for the storage complex.
