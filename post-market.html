C. Macro Day Review

1. The Iran-diplomacy headline — intraday reversal engine, then credibly contested P1 credible-unconfirmed, leaning contested

Time: headline around 10:00 ET; pushback mid-afternoon ET.

Confirmed: Al-Araby Al-Jadeed reported mediators had presented a proposal to revive U.S.-Iran talks, and the report moved markets exactly as described (SPX from red to +0.7%, WTI about $100 to about $95.30, Brent -1.6%, 10Y and DXY to session lows). Via Israel's Channel 12 citing the Qatari report: the proposal was delivered by Pakistan's army chief/Field Marshal Asim Munir to senior Iranian officials in Tehran on Tuesday; reported terms included transferring interest on about $6B Iranian funds frozen in Qatari banks and a confidential, unannounced IAEA visit to Iranian nuclear facilities, with Qatar and Pakistan as mediators.

Not confirmed: no endorsement from Washington or Tehran by the close. Iran's SNSC secretary Mohsen Rezaei reportedly told Munir there would be no negotiations unless Washington meets Iran's conditions; Reuters' Parisa Hafezi reported three senior Iranian officials saying Tehran is deliberately dragging talks out to deny the U.S. president a pre-election win and is preparing for renewed Israeli/U.S. attacks, not a deal. Regime-affiliated outlets (Tabnak/Fars) say Rezaei refused to meet Munir and the visit was nearly scrapped — finalized Monday at noon as oil pushed toward $110, described as meant to calm markets, not signal a breakthrough. Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid in Tuesday's morning note: "Markets have heard this story before and will take a lot to believe it."

Why important: the intraday whipsaw was the session's dominant macro driver; WTI's partial rebound into the close tracks the pushback timeline. An unconfirmed headline that moves front-month oil about 5% is a direct repricing event for energy-linked vol and for any premium-selling calibrated on morning prints.
