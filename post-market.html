A. Session Recap

Monday was a rebound day, led by technology and semiconductors, after Friday's broad selloff. The AI-financing debate did not go away — it changed shape: instead of selling AI stocks on funding fears, the market bought the enablers (chips, memory, power) on the biggest AI capital-raising day of the year, while selling the funders (Meta) on dilution fears. Oil pulled back, the 10-year yield sat exactly on 5.00%, and Brazil suffered a historic political repricing that stayed mostly contained to Brazilian assets.

C. Macro Day Review

1. The AI capital-raising blitz — three deals in one morning

Confirmed Anthropic filed its S-1 for a Nasdaq IPO under the ticker ANT, the first frontier AI lab to file publicly. The prospectus showed first-half 2026 revenue of $16.29 billion versus $1.2 billion a year earlier — roughly 13-fold growth — alongside a first-half net loss of $80.7 billion, most of it a $72.4 billion non-cash charge from revaluing convertible notes as Anthropic's valuation rose; the operating loss was $693 million, and the second quarter alone earned $1.0 billion in operating income. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead 22 underwriters. Credible but unconfirmed: the valuation (reports put it at $1.5 trillion or more, up to $2 trillion) and the raise size (reported up to ~$100 billion), with a listing possibly as soon as mid-November. Sources: Reuters Breakingviews, AI Stock Wire S-1 summary, Motley Fool.

Confirmed Meta announced plans to raise up to $95 billion in its largest-ever equity fundraising: about $35 billion of common and preferred stock offerings launched on Monday, plus a separate at-the-market program for up to $60 billion of non-voting shares. This resolves the conflicting $22.5B-versus-$95B figures that circulated in the pre-market edition — the $95B total is the correct frame. Meta shares fell 1.88%. Source: Reuters.

Confirmed SpaceX struck a ~$40 billion deal with an Apollo Global Management affiliate, which will buy AI chips and server hardware and lease them to SpaceX (total principal payments about $40 billion). Separately, Anthropic agreed to spend up to $120.5 billion with SpaceX's AI subsidiary on computing capacity running into 2029 — about $3.1 billion a month once all capacity is delivered, expected December 2026 — though either side can cancel on 90 days' notice after initial periods ending in early 2027. Source: Reuters; AI Stock Wire.

Analysis: Monday's tape split the AI complex in two. Companies that supply the buildout (Nvidia, Micron, SK Hynix, Bloom Energy) rallied; companies that pay for it with new shares (Meta) fell. That is a healthier discrimination than last week's indiscriminate selling, but it also means the financing question is now priced name by name — and the next test is whether Microsoft and Alphabet can show the cash flow to match their spending on Wednesday evening.

2. Brazil: a historic political repricing

Confirmed Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won Sunday's runoff — the first candidate ever to win the decisive second round after losing the first — taking a record fourth term (he turns 81 on Tuesday). Markets had priced a win for Flávio Bolsonaro, so the unwind was violent: the Ibovespa fell 13.88% to 176,608 (B3 paused trading for 30 minutes), the US-listed Brazil ETF EWZ fell 19.82%, and Petrobras fell 13.99% in the Fool's close print. Credible but unconfirmed / conflicting: the real's fall is reported between −6.60% and −8.5% depending on the source (Reuters calls −8.5% the biggest fall since the 1999 devaluation), and some morning tables showed steeper ADR prints (XP −45% to −48%, Itaú −20% to −27%, Bradesco −28% to −31%) — the close prints are the ones to use, and the dispersion itself is a warning about single-source intraday tables. Sources: Reuters Trading Day; Strategitz. Analysis: contagion to US equities was limited — this read as a Brazil-specific position unwind, not a global risk event — but it is a live reminder that election outcomes still reprice whole markets in a day.

3. Oil, rates and the Fed

Oil fell as Saudi Arabia's defense minister held talks in Tehran, easing some of last week's Strait-of-Hormuz premium: WTI settled at $101.76 (−1.46%), Brent's December contract at $115.81 (−0.78%). Note the contract confusion in intraday coverage (a Brent futures print near $110.32 circulated alongside the $115.81 December settle) — the settle figures above are the consistent series. The 10-year yield held at 5.00%, the 2-year eased 1 bp to 4.68%, the 30-year slipped 2 bp to 5.31%, leaving 2s10s at +32 bp. A US 2-year auction was described as OK, with foreign demand dipping and domestic demand surging. The Fed meets October 27–28; Reuters, citing CME FedWatch, reports markets price only a 12.7% chance of a rate rise at Wednesday's meeting, and Goldman is reported to expect a skip. Chair Warsh speaks at 14:30 ET on Wednesday, after the 14:00 ET decision. Analysis: with the 10-year pinned at 5.00% and a 5% handle acting as a valuation ceiling, Wednesday's press conference matters as much as the decision itself — a hawkish hold would hit exactly the long-duration AI spenders that rallied Monday.

4. Overnight Asia into Monday

Overnight Asia was mixed before the US open: Japan's Nikkei fell 0.80% on chip selling; Korea's KOSPI fell 1.46% with net foreign selling of ₩816.5 billion; Shanghai rose 0.19% and Hong Kong 0.51%. In Seoul on Monday morning, SK Hynix rose 0.8% to ₩1,287,000 ahead of Wednesday's earnings while Samsung slipped 0.5%, and Goldman Sachs cut its 12-month KOSPI target to 9,000 from 12,000 — while noting that target still implies more than 60% upside, arguing the market "is pricing an overly negative fundamental outlook." Source: Aju Press.

G. Short-Option Strategy Review (analysis only — read-only, no trading action taken)

Environment change: Volatility compressed (VIX 19.45 → 18.39, UVXY −3.57%) but did not collapse — implied volatility still sits above realized volatility (~10–12%), so premium sellers are still paid for event risk, but less than on Friday. The 3x complex rallied hard (SOXL +8.31%, TECL +5.01%, TQQQ +3.23%, KORU +2.93%, LABU +1.77%), which cuts both ways: it rebuilds premium for call spreads, but selling calls into a +8% day means selling into momentum, not into exhaustion.

By name, against the standing playbook

  • KORU — default stays bear call spread. Playbook parameters: spreads only, 14–30 DTE, far out-of-the-money strikes (10–15 delta), $3–5 wings, max loss 1–2% per trade, take profit at 50–70% of credit. Monday's US rally against Seoul's −1.46% overnight session is exactly the two-way gap risk the playbook warns about: a name that can gap either way overnight should not be sold aggressively before SK Hynix's Wednesday earnings and the Fed decision. The cleaner version of the view, per the playbook, is to sell the rip after the event wall, not to chase Monday's rip — and never a naked short, never a cash-secured put/wheel on a decaying 3x.
  • LABU — trend supports put spreads, timing argues patience. LABU's uptrend (YTD ~+40%) makes put spreads the with-trend expression, and Monday's +1.77% fits that script. But the same Wednesday wall applies: a Fed surprise or a Microsoft/Alphabet miss would hit biotech beta too. Small size or post-wall entry keeps the 1–2% max-loss rule intact.
  • SOXL / TECL / TQQQ — do not sell strength blindly. After +8.31% / +5.01% / +3.23% days, call spreads sold Monday evening start life behind momentum; put spreads sold into the same strength face Thursday's GDP/PCE and SanDisk earnings. The playbook's discipline — wide wings, far strikes, 14–30 DTE, 50–70% profit-taking — matters more than direction here, because Thursday's data can reverse Monday's tape in one print.
  • UVXY — volatility crush is not a strategy. UVXY −3.57% is the expected mirror of VIX −5.45%, but the product's contango decay and the still-elevated VIX (18.39) argue for the playbook's narrow treatment: short-DTE bear call spreads only, or avoidance, sized small into the Fed.
  • Single names — event-specific caution. SNDK earnings Thursday and SKHY earnings Wednesday make short premium on those names a binary bet unless wings are wide and strikes far; MU's post-ex-dividend early-assignment note from the playbook (October 14 ex-date, $0.15) is a standing reminder to check dividend dates before holding short deep-in-the-money calls. MSTR/GDX/SPCX stay in the wide-wing, far-strike, event-driven bucket only.

Tomorrow / this-week outlook (analysis): If Wednesday delivers a hold with a calm press conference plus solid Microsoft/Alphabet prints, implied volatility should compress further and the post-wall premium-selling window the playbook prefers opens on Thursday — but Thursday itself carries GDP, PCE, Amazon and SanDisk, so the truly clean window may be Friday, not Thursday morning. If Wednesday disappoints on either leg, Monday's rebound gives back quickly and the same spreads benefit from the vol expansion only if they were sized small and struck far. Either way, the rule that survives both paths is the playbook's: spreads only, 1–2% max loss, 50–70% profit-taking, no naked shorts, no chasing.
