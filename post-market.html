C. Macro Day Review (Detailed)

1. The Fed: hold this week, hawkish voices on Friday

The Federal Reserve left its target range at 3.75%–4.00% at its October 27–28 meeting, with two members dissenting in favour of hiking immediately. The statement described activity expanding "at a strong pace", domestic spending resilient, productivity strong and capital investment robust, while "inflation remains elevated" and the Committee will "deliver price stability". Chair Kevin Warsh said the Fed will be data-led rather than guiding markets to a preordained path, and that the Fed will hold eight meetings in 2027 — one focused solely on broad monetary-policy strategy — and six rate meetings in 2028. Confirmed fact

Friday brought the first round of Fed commentary after the meeting, and it leaned hawkish. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan (a 2026 FOMC voter) called the level and persistence of inflation "unacceptable" and said "it remains clear to me… that the (FOMC) needs to implement at least two more 25-basis-point rate increases". St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem (non-voter in 2026) also argued a hike is needed soon. Richmond Fed President Thomas Barkin backed gradual rate hikes at midday and named AI spending an inflation risk. San Francisco's Mary Daly also spoke at noon. Futures markets eased back on December hike expectations during the day, but still price a hike as more likely than a hold — around 70% odds of a 25 bp December hike (CME FedWatch via Invezz). Confirmed fact (Reuters, Oct 30)

Market impact: the hawkish chorus added a policy reason to sell long-duration tech on top of the earnings-driven one. The 10-year yield nevertheless fell 6 bp to 4.93% — the risk-off bid into Treasuries outweighed the hawkish repricing at the long end, while the front end stayed pinned by hike pricing. Analysis

2. Growth strong, inflation sticky — the uncomfortable combination

  • Q3 GDP grew 3.4% annualised, with consumer spending supportive — the economy's resilience is not in doubt. Confirmed fact
  • Inflation is proving harder to contain: the Fed's preferred PCE measure is running at 3.6%, core PCE at 3.1% — both well above the 2% target. Confirmed fact
  • Employment Cost Index was released at 8:30 a.m. ET Friday (BLS) — the morning's scheduled labour-cost read. Confirmed fact
  • Chicago PMI: the prices-paid component hit its highest since December 2021, a fresh input-cost warning on the same morning the market was already selling tech. Confirmed fact (midday brief)
  • Jobs backdrop: September payrolls grew just 29,000 with unemployment at 4.2% — the October report (Friday Nov 6) now carries more weight than usual. Confirmed fact

Read-through: strong growth plus sticky inflation plus a Fed that is openly discussing more hikes is the "higher-for-longer" configuration. It punishes exactly the assets that need cheap, abundant capital to justify their valuations — long-duration AI capex stories — while rewarding cash-rich, cash-generative businesses. That rotation was visible all week: bond proxies and cash-heavy names held up while the AI complex fell. Analysis

3. Earnings: the breadth says "fine", the mega-caps say "prove it"

Through October 30, 299 S&P 500 companies had reported Q3 results and 82% beat consensus (LSEG data via Reuters). The season is not a broad earnings failure — it is a concentrated one. The market is no longer rewarding companies simply for announcing larger AI investments; it is demanding evidence of an attractive return on that capital (Massey Romans). Alphabet lost about $500B of market value in a single day after its report; Microsoft, by contrast, was rewarded for Azure +48% and $18.6B of free cash flow. Confirmed fact

4. Geopolitics and commodities: oil above $100, gold liquidated

  • Oil stayed above $100 after an attack on a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz. WTI $102.95 (−0.21% on the day), Brent $112.33 (+0.23%). Energy was the day's best sector (+1.91%); Exxon and Chevron held their gains in cash trade (+1.7% and +1.6% at midday). Confirmed fact
  • Gold fell 2.97% to $3,884.90 and silver 4.29% to $49.13 on the same day Treasuries rallied — an unusual pairing that points to broad liquidation rather than a simple flight to safety. High-yield spreads widened ~10–12 bp to ~323–326 bp. Confirmed fact
  • Binance hack: news of a massive hack at cryptocurrency exchange Binance hit crypto during the session; bitcoin traded below $77,000, down from near $82,000 the prior day, and crypto-related stocks tumbled. Confirmed fact (Investopedia)
  • U.S. midterms, Tuesday Nov 3: prediction markets point to a Republican-controlled Senate and a Democrat-controlled House as the most likely outcome; voters' discontent over inflation is a central driver. Credible but unconfirmed — prediction-market pricing, not a result

Sources: Reuters via WDSM — Fed officials make case for rate hikes; Invezz — a hawkish Fed, shaky tech; CWS Market Review, Oct 30; Massey Romans — In the Loop, Oct 30; Investopedia.

D. Sector Day Review (by watchlist industry group)

1. Semiconductors / Memory — the epicentre (MU, NVDA, AMD, SNDK, SKHY, SOXL)

Worst group of the day by a wide margin. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell roughly 8–10% intraday (SOXX −8.59% at midday, SMH −7.0%); the technology sector lost 4.24% (The Edge) — a second source prints −4.89% (Pulsafi); see Data Gaps. The trigger was Intel's Q4 guidance: revenue $15–16B and EPS $0.25, both below expectations, with management citing continued PC softness, rising memory prices weighing on PC demand, and an accident at its Israeli plant constraining supply. Intel fell 28.58% to $65.76 — its worst day since the 1970s (CNBC/AP). The warning reverberated across the whole complex because it hit the demand side of the AI story: if memory prices are choking PC demand and AI chip demand is starving the PC business, the memory supercycle itself comes into question. SanDisk fell 13.5% despite beating earnings, guiding up and announcing an 8-for-1 split — analysts flagged valuation after a ~337–385% YTD rally, and BofA cut its price target to $2,000 from $2,500 (Buy retained). SK Hynix fell 7.95% in the U.S. even though it reported record Q3 results two days earlier (operating profit up 632% YoY, ~80% margin); Seoul-listed SK Hynix fell 7.08% and Samsung 3.02% on Friday, and the Korea Exchange triggered a rare five-minute sell-side sidecar halt after KOSPI 200 futures fell 5.12%. Confirmed fact

Counterweight: the SOX index is now described as oversold and bottoming at its 200-day moving average dating back to July (Massey Romans), and the group is being watched for a hold-and-reverse into year-end. That is a technical observation, not a confirmed turn. Analysis

2. AI compute chain (NVDA, AMD, PLTR, BE, MSFT, META, GOOG, AVGO)

The AI compute chain was the day's second-worst pocket, and the driver was the same cash-flow question. Amazon reported a third consecutive quarter of negative free cash flow while maintaining its $220B capex plan for 2026, with spending expected to rise further next year; the stock fell ~1.9% after recovering most of a much deeper pre-market gap. Alphabet's Thursday collapse (−12.64% to $283.82) set the tone: Q3 revenue $127.6B (+25%) missed the ~$128.2B estimate, adjusted EPS $2.67 missed the ~$3.02 target, free cash flow burned −$9.1B after $51.4B of capex, and management held its 2026 capex range of $195–205B while signalling a significant step-up in 2027. Microsoft was the relative outperformer in the group (−1.55% Friday) after Azure +48% and ~$18.6B of free cash flow — the market's split verdict on the hyperscalers is now explicit: spending is not disqualifying, but the cash has to keep flowing. Palantir fell 6.27% into its Monday earnings; Bloom Energy fell 8.61% with the AI-power trade. Broadcom fell 4.1% and Arm 10.2% (KuCoin/RootData intraday prints). Confirmed fact

3. Big tech / Consumer tech (AAPL, MSFT, META, GOOG, TSLA, APP, NVDA, TECL, TQQQ)

4. Biotech (LABU, MRNA)

Biotech held up far better than tech. LABU fell 3.79% to $247.22 — a bad day in absolute terms, but less than half the Nasdaq 100's drop and far better than the semiconductor 3x. Moderna was near-flat at −0.47% ($221.72), its October +17.35% and YTD +651.85% runs intact. Health care was one of the six rising S&P sectors on the day. The biotech 3x remains the year's genuine bull story: LABU is +55.1% YTD and +88.65% over one year (Finnhub). Confirmed fact

Korea was hit from both sides: Thursday's U.S. tech selloff and Friday's Seoul session. The KOSPI closed 3.48% lower at 5,730.74 on Friday with the rare sidecar trading curb triggered; SK Hynix (Seoul) −7.08%, Samsung −3.02%.
