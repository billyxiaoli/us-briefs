D. Sector Day Review (by watchlist industry groups)

1. Semiconductors / Memory (MU, NVDA, AMD, SNDK, SKHY, SOXL)

SOX −6.72% to 10,731.98 — worst day since June 23, lowest since July 29; all 30 constituents declined. Three converging shocks: (a) Lam Research −11.76% — record Q1 revenue but Dec-quarter guidance (~$8.6B) dented by supply-chain and clean-room constraints extending into 2027; analysts cut targets, calling it supply-constrained not demand-constrained; (b) CXMT "4F square" DRAM progress (announced Oct 19) stoking China competition/pricing fears; Samsung (286-layer Xi'an) and SK Hynix (240L+ Dalian) are upgrading China NAND lines; Micron's CTO downplayed CXMT's design as "complex and expensive"; (c) a 24-hour warning strike at Micron's Taoyuan DRAM fab (9:00 Taiwan time) over profit sharing — only ~11 union officials joined; Micron announced a quarterly profit-linked bonus program the same day.

Memory fundamentals vs price action: the de-rating continued against intact pricing — 1b DRAM now earns ~$0.654/mm² (54% more per wafer area than TSMC 2nm logic); DDR5 contract prices +83% since March; TrendForce sees Q4 NAND contract prices +15–20% QoQ and enterprise SSD +23–28% QoQ; Kioxia says all 2026 output is sold out; Micron expects constraints through 2027–28, with >75% of CY27 output committed and 2027 HBM prices resetting "significantly higher" Jan 1. SK Hynix fell 24% in the Oct 12–16 week (worst since 2008); Oct 22 extended it −8.07%. The memory value story is strong; the equity story is de-rating on capex/supply fears — a divergence worth watching, not resolving tonight.

Power, not chips, is the AI bottleneck: Microsoft walked away (~Oct 19) from the Nscale Loughton UK campus (23,040 GB300 GPUs) because the grid couldn't deliver power until the early-to-mid 2030s; Jensen Huang (GTC Berlin, Oct 22) unveiled the Vera Rubin-powered DSX POD (6-month → 14-day deployment) but said neocloud demand is "off the charts" while capital is the binding constraint, and that US export rules "definitely accelerated" China's chip push.
