F. Short-Option Strategy Update

Read-only analysis, not a trade order. No trade was placed, modified, or recommended in this brief. Framework from the standing playbook (~/workspace/us-stock-reports/option-strategy-playbook.md): spreads only — never naked short puts/calls; max loss 1–2% of account per trade; DTE 14–30 days; exit at 50–70% of credit; direction follows market state (trend × volatility regime).

  • Volatility regime today: compressing, but event risk is priced in. UVXY −5.70% and the equity rebound point to falling short-term vol expectations, yet the S&P weekly straddle is priced ~1.80% (~15% wider than last week) — the market is paying up for Wednesday's FOMC + MSFT/GOOG prints and Thursday's GDP/PCE. That combination (spot vol down, event vol up) argues against adding new short premium into Wednesday: the premium on offer today partly compensates for a known binary, not for quiet conditions.
  • KORU — no clean signal; do not anchor on today's print. The playbook default is bear call spreads (extreme vol, overnight gap risk, 63% of days moving >±5%). Today's disputed +4.30% print cannot be used to judge trend or IV, and the overnight KOSPI actually fell −1.46%. With SK hynix earnings Wednesday and a possible (unconfirmed) dividend announcement, the known-overnight-event rule applies: reduce or tighten Korea exposure into Wednesday rather than adding. Confirm KORU's real price and its listed strikes/months in the broker app before any first trade — do not assume weeklies exist.
  • LABU — trend still friendly to put spreads, with a caveat. LABU +2.87% today, +13.6% over 5 days, YTD-positive trend year (playbook: +41.6% YTD). In a trend market, put spreads collect both direction and decay; call spreads risk being run over (June's +60.8% month is the cautionary example). But LABU is −10.52% over the past month and biotech catalysts cluster this week — a put spread entered today would carry event risk into Wednesday–Friday prints. The playbook's “sell the rip / sell after a pullback steadies” discipline matters more than today's green print.
  • SOXL / TECL / TQQQ — a +5–10% leveraged up day is a warning, not an invitation. A 3× ETF that gains 10% in a morning can give back a similar amount on one bad earnings print (MSFT/GOOG Wednesday, AMZN Thursday). Playbook parameters for these names are LABU-level (wings slightly narrower than KORU's). After a sharp rebound day, the natural playbook-consistent posture is to let the move settle and sell premium only on a steadied tape with 14–30 DTE — not to chase the morning's highs with short calls that a Wednesday beat could run over, nor to sell puts into an FOMC decision that could gap the underlying.
  • UVXY — decay is working, but that is not a strategy by itself. Contango decay favours bear call spreads on UVXY, but only at short DTE and with the usual spread discipline; a vol spike (e.g., a hawkish Warsh message Wednesday) can reprice UVXY violently. Today's −5.70% is a good day for existing bear-call positions, not a reason to size up.
  • Single names — event-specific notes. MU/NVDA/AMD/SNDK: memory strength helps existing short-put structures, but SNDK's earnings date is itself disputed (Oct 28 vs 29) — never hold a short spread through an earnings print whose date you have not confirmed. META: the $35B offering plus Wednesday earnings make this a two-event week; premium is rich for a reason. MRNA: wide intraday range ($181.65–203.82) after last week's crash — realised vol is still extreme; spreads need wider wings and smaller size, if anything. MSTR: the STRC-buyback story (not BTC price) is today's driver — a reminder that MSTR's equity can diverge from BTC for days.
  • Close-of-session risk reminder. The 1:00 p.m. ET $69B 2-year auction, the CDNS/WELL/NUE/CLS/AMKR earnings after the close, and any late headline on Iran mediation can all reprice the tape before the close. Existing positions should be checked against their 50–70% profit-taking levels before the close rather than carried unexamined into Wednesday's FOMC.
