F. Short-Option Strategy Update

Read-only analysis. This section analyses spread strategy under the standing playbook (spreads only, never naked; max loss 1–2% per trade; DTE 14–30 days; take profit at 50–70% of credit). It is not an order, not a recommendation to open or close any specific contract, and no trading action has been taken. Contract specifics (available months, strike spacing, weeklies) must be confirmed in the broker app before any decision — especially for KORU.

Volatility regime: calm session, loaded calendar

VIX ~17.80 intraday, 1-day gauge 10.49, Friday expiry priced at only 104.90 SPX points (1.37%) versus 135.65 a day earlier (Saxo). Premium has already compressed into an event stack (FOMC Wednesday, MSFT/GOOGL Wednesday after the close, SNDK/MSTR Thursday, SKHY Wednesday). Compressed premium cuts both ways for a short seller: less credit on offer, and a surprise move has an outsized percentage impact on the spread. The playbook's discipline — far out-of-the-money strikes, wide-enough wings, 14–30 DTE — matters more than usual this week; short-dated, tight spreads are where a Fed/earnings gap hurts most.

KORU / SKHY — the Korea leg is working, and that is the risk

KORU +7.54% on a KOSPI +3.1–3.75% day is the leveraged beta doing its job. Playbook default remains bear call spread (collect decay, defend against the overnight gap) — and today is a textbook “sell the rip” setup only in the analytical sense: after a +7.5% rip, upside strikes are further away, but a second strong Seoul session (SKHY Q3 Wednesday, plus the unconfirmed special-dividend story) can keep running. Known overnight-event rule applies: do not assume a quiet Seoul night before SKHY's print. Wing width $3–5 and 10–15 delta far-OTM strikes remain the playbook frame; do not tighten wings to chase credit after a rip, and never run a cash-secured put / wheel here (assignment = catching a decaying 3×).

LABU — trend intact, MRNA is noise inside the ETF today

LABU +5.59% while MRNA fell sharply is a useful reminder that the ETF is not one stock. Playbook: LABU (89.5% annualised vol, no overnight gap — all constituents trade in US hours) allows put spreads more comfortably than KORU, with 15–20 delta strikes and slightly narrower wings. In a year-to-date uptrend (+41.6% YTD per the playbook), put spreads ride trend + decay; call spreads are the side that gets squeezed in a +60%-month type move. Today's MRNA downgrade is a stock-specific valuation call, not a biotech-sector break — it argues for keeping LABU spreads diversified in strike terms rather than re-aiming them at MRNA.

SOXL / TECL / TQQQ — momentum with an earnings cliff

SOXL +8.85%, TECL +4.65%, TQQQ +3.38%: all three are being carried by the same Anthropic/memory impulse. Playbook treatment is LABU-grade (wings slightly narrower than KORU, direction by trend and vol state). Into the close, the binding constraint is not today's move but tomorrow's: MSFT/GOOGL after the close Wednesday, SNDK Thursday. A short spread opened today at compressed premium carries a full earnings gap for little credit — the analytical signal is to wait for the post-earnings volatility reset rather than to add size into it. If existing spreads are already near 50–70% of credit, the playbook's take-profit rule says take it; do not hold for the last slice through a Fed decision.

UVXY — no clean quote, no new signal

With VIX falling, UVXY-type exposure is directionally down, and contango (front-month futures 19.12 vs spot ~17.80) still favours decay over time. Playbook: only short-DTE bear call spreads or avoidance. No UVXY % is quoted today (see §I), so no position-level inference is drawn from it.
