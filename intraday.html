Memory is that trade — SK Hynix's record quarter carried KORU +13.6% and SKHY +12.1% intraday, with Micron +4.7% and SanDisk +5.5% riding along — while the broad indexes are essentially flat (SPY +0.16%, QQQ +0.10%) and most of the watchlist's big names are fading their pre-market gains (Tesla −1.5%, Meta −1.2%, Palantir −2.4%, Microsoft −0.5%). Trade the session as positioning into those prints, not as a verdict on them.

C. Macro News Intraday (Detailed)

1. The Fed is still the whole afternoon: hold priced, December and dissents are the trade

The FOMC decision lands at 14:00 ET, with Chair Warsh's press conference at 14:30 ET. The target range is 3.75–4.00% after September's 25bp hike (12–0 vote, the Fed's first increase since July 2023). A hold is ~90–94% priced (Reuters poll: 94 of 100 economists expect no change; CME FedWatch ~90% hold, ~10% hike). All 100 economists in that poll expect a December hike, while futures price roughly 71–83% for December — that gap, plus any dissent for a hike, is what the statement and press conference can move. This is a non-SEP meeting (no dot plot), so Warsh's words carry the signal; he is noted for offering little forward guidance. September data frame the dilemma: CPI 3.7% year over year (core 2.6%), payrolls +29,000, and energy CPI +20.4% year over year. Source: PipTheory · Source: CoinDesk · Source: Forex.com

2. Bank of Canada hiked this morning — the first hike since July 2023, and the reason matters for the Fed read

The Bank of Canada raised its policy rate 25bp to 2.50% on Wednesday morning, its first increase since July 2023. Governor Macklem's statement: with the Middle East war persisting, global oil prices have increased and are expected to remain higher for longer, hitting Canadians at the pump and raising business costs; with the inflation outlook "substantially higher" and the economy resilient, a hike was needed to reduce the risk that elevated energy prices spill over into generalised inflation. Canada's September inflation was 3.1%, driven almost entirely by energy, while core measures eased below 2%. A majority of economists in a Reuters poll also forecast one more BoC rise before March. This is not a Fed signal by itself — but it is the clearest live example of an energy-inflation hawkish pivot, and it arrived hours before Warsh speaks. Source: Reuters (Macklem statement) · Source: Reuters

3. Oil: the premium is holding into midday, on a claim still unconfirmed by Riyadh

WTI is ~$101.75 intraday (+~2%), Brent ~$115.60, after WTI fell ~1.8% Tuesday to $99.90. The trigger remains the Houthi claim of a missile hit on Saudi Arabia's largest refinery (reported as Ras Tanura, ~550,000 bpd) on Tuesday. Unconfirmed: Saudi authorities had not confirmed the strike at the time of writing — the oil spike is on the claim plus the risk premium, not on a confirmed outage. Oil's rise is also the macro thread tying the BoC hike, the 10Y at ~5.01%, and the Fed's inflation problem together. Source: Investopedia · Source: Kitco / Reuters
